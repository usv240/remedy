# Results, in full

Part of the [Remedy README](../README.md). Every figure here is counted by code from a file in the repo; `tests/facts.test.ts` fails when this page disagrees with [`content/public/facts.json`](../content/public/facts.json).

## The data behind it

The CPSC's own wording, from recall 27056: *"submit a photo of their recalled power bank with the word 'recalled' written on it in permanent marker"*.

- **What each photo is for.** 525 recalls since January 2024 name a photo and a refund in their remedy (the classifier in [`src/lib/recall/classify.ts`](../src/lib/recall/classify.ts)). Their proof rules then say what the photo must show ([`src/lib/recall/made-safe.ts`](../src/lib/recall/made-safe.ts), counts in [`content/recalls/proof-split.json`](../content/recalls/proof-split.json)): 452 ask for a photo of the product made safe, of which 447 pay a refund for it (the headline) and 5 pay store credit or a gift card; 71 ask for a photo for another reason, 68 of the product as it is (proof of ownership) and 3 of a label, lot or date code; 2 need no photo once read closely. The 71 still fit the checks: Made safe passes when the rule asks for no change, and Within the recall reads a lot or date code from the photo and compares it with the range the recall states.
- **What is at stake.** The 447 recalls offer $471.1 million in refunds: each one's US units times the lowest sale price its record states, or the fixed refund its rule states when that is lower (442 counted; 3 pro-rated refunds state no share and 2 records state no price, so those 5 are left out). Of it, $261.2 million to $442.8 million stays unclaimed, at the CPSC's FY2025 average correction rate (44.55%) and at about 6 in 100 units coming back; each extra point of return sends $4.7 million back to owners. The unclaimed range is an inference: it applies rates measured across recalls to these recalls' dollars, not a count of unpaid refunds. The two largest recalls, 24309 and 25394, are $184.0 million of the $471.1 million (39.1%), and the median recall offers $68,000 ([`content/recalls/value.json`](../content/recalls/value.json)).
- 498 of the 525 list an email address for the claim, and 270 of them list a free webmail address.
- 8 of the 525 ask for more than one photo (recall 25007 asks for two: the straps cut off, and the helmet in the trash). Remedy reads the count from the remedy text in code ([`src/lib/recall/photo-count.ts`](../src/lib/recall/photo-count.ts)); the claim page then takes that many photos, each gets the free checks, and all of them go to Claude in one request.
- None of the 1,239 recalls mentions PayPal or Venmo.
- 81% of all recalls (1,002 of 1,239) cover fewer than 50,000 units: mostly small firms, handling each photo by hand.

The same classifier, run on the earlier snapshot behind our research note (1,227 recalls to October 29, 2026), gives the same 523 photo-refund recalls it reported.

## Results

- **Reading the remedy, benchmarked (draft labels).** 130 real CPSC remedies (100 photo-refund, 30 not), seven fields each. Claude Opus 5.5 with a self-check got every field right on **113 of 130**; one Claude pass, 110 of 130; keyword rules written the way an engineer would start, 73 of 130. The labels are a draft: a second, more careful Claude pass with a deterministic cross-check, not yet confirmed by a person, so Claude's scores partly measure agreement between two Claude passes ([`content/recalls/benchmark/results.json`](../content/recalls/benchmark/results.json)).
- **Is it recalled? Snap the label, measured in code.** An owner photographs a product's label; Claude reads brand, product, model numbers and UPC, each with the text it was read from, and code looks them up with the catalog scan's matcher, Claude off: a model number or UPC in a recall's own list settles it ([`src/lib/label/check.ts`](../src/lib/label/check.ts)). Over all 1,239 recalls, the 516 that list a model number or UPC gave 2,216 owner-style readings: brand and model (814 of 821 found), the model with spaces, dashes or case changed (1,289 of 1,297), the UPC alone (81 of 81) and the UPC without its leading zero (17 of 17). Found the right recall for 2,201 of 2,216 label readings; 511 of 511 not-recalled products matched no recall. (147 of the found readings carry a code that more than one recall lists, so the owner picks; the negatives are each recall's model with its digits changed, 483 of 483, and 28 everyday products, 28 of 28.) No AI call; [`content/public/label-eval.json`](../content/public/label-eval.json) by [`scripts/label/eval-catalog.ts`](../scripts/label/eval-catalog.ts). A photo eval of 30 real label pictures, with the recall each must find written down first ([`content/label/photo-eval-cases.json`](../content/label/photo-eval-cases.json)), runs with `scripts/label/eval-photos.ts` under a spend cap, about $0.77.
- **Every photo-refund remedy, read once.** 583 proof rules (all 525 photo-refund recalls, 30 recent recalls without a photo step and 28 more from the benchmark) in one Message Batches run: 1,166 calls, $14.01 at batch prices, 6.9 minutes ([`content/recalls/proof-rules.json`](../content/recalls/proof-rules.json)).
- **Photos that need a closer look are found before any AI call.** The recall's official picture, rotated and cropped copies of it, a photo already used for a claim, a blurry photo, and a picture published with a different recall: **7 of 7** stopped by code alone, 6 asked for a new photo and the photo already used sent to a person at the firm. They are the 7 of the error rates' 50 decisions settled in code; the model made the other 43. The hash comparison itself takes a median of 2 ms (0.2 to 93.8 ms); reading the photo and answering takes a median of 98 ms (55 to 200 ms), across 7 recorded runs with no model call. A photo of a mug sent for the power bank recall scored 0.02 on the right-product check and was not paid ([`content/public/verify-runs.json`](../content/public/verify-runs.json)).
- **One photo, two firms.** A photo already claimed at Brightline Home is sent for Fernway Home's recall (both fictional sample firms) and held for a person before any model call, because its 64-bit hash matches Brightline's claim; only the hash and a row id cross. With Brightline's hash left out, the same photo goes on to the model step ([`content/public/network-run.json`](../content/public/network-run.json), recorded by [`scripts/public/record-network-run.ts`](../scripts/public/record-network-run.ts)).
- **Another firm can join the pool.** Two documented calls, `POST /api/v1/hash-pool/register` and `POST /api/v1/hash-pool/check`, were made with curl from outside the app against the local build: a self-serve sandbox key was refused (403) when it tried to register, since only a key tied to a connected firm may write to the pool; the sample charger's hash registered with Brightline's firm key was a hit at 0 bits when asked for Fernway's, with no key, and a photo of a mug was not; only the hash and an opaque ref crossed ([`content/public/hash-pool-call.json`](../content/public/hash-pool-call.json), recorded by [`scripts/public/record-hash-pool-call.ts`](../scripts/public/record-hash-pool-call.ts); `tests/hash-pool.test.ts` runs the same comparison with no database).
- **The same hash against a reused photo.** The 9 photos with a known answer, each put through 13 changes a reuser makes (saved again at three qualities, halved and quartered, cropped, turned a few degrees, brightened and darkened, screenshotted on a phone, mirrored), give 117 copies. With both hashes at the thresholds the claim route uses, either hash catches 108 of 117 with 0 unrelated matches, so **108 of 117** are held across firms, and only the phone screenshots get past both. The second hash, of the photo's center ([`src/lib/verify/phash.ts`](../src/lib/verify/phash.ts)), catches 108 of 117 at 6 bits on its own (its nearest unrelated picture is 8 bits away). The difference hash alone, against the source photo's stored hash, catches **74 of 117** at 4 bits and **85 of 117** at 10 bits: every re-saved, resized, brightened and mirrored copy, most 4 percent crops and 2 degree turns, and none of the 10 percent crops, 5 degree turns or phone screenshots. Against every other recall's distinct published pictures (1,776 for the sample charger's photos: the catalog's 1,779 across all recalls, less the sample recall's own 3) and the other 8 photos, **0 of 117** copies come within 4 bits of a picture they are not a copy of, and 23 come within 10, which is why the wider threshold is used only within one recall ([`content/public/hash-reuse-run.json`](../content/public/hash-reuse-run.json), recorded by [`scripts/public/hash-reuse-run.ts`](../scripts/public/hash-reuse-run.ts); `tests/hash-reuse-run.test.ts` makes the copies again).
- **The pool, in short.** Measured on 117 reused copies of 9 photos: the two hashes together catch 108 of 117 across firms, with 0 unrelated matches; the difference hash alone catches 74 at its 4-bit threshold across firms (85 at 10 bits), with 0 unrelated matches. Only the phone screenshot copies get past both. Matching by hash alone is proven by [Microsoft PhotoDNA](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/photodna) and [StopNCII](https://stopncii.org/how-it-works/); Remedy's pool is open code, holds recall photos, and shares only two 64-bit hashes and a row id.
- **A replay of the pattern on the CPSC's docket.** One device filing over 400 claims, replayed in code (not the real claims): 400 altered copies of one claimed photo, filed by turns at two fictional sample firms. The pool held **308 of 400** before any model call; the 78 that went on to the model step are all phone screenshots, and none of the 400 was paid ([`content/public/docket-replay.json`](../content/public/docket-replay.json), recorded by [`scripts/public/docket-replay.ts`](../scripts/public/docket-replay.ts); `tests/docket-replay.test.ts` files the first claims again).
- **One reviewer against the referee.** Of 49 recorded model decisions at the claim path's setting, 7 reached the two reviewers. (The 49 are the 42 decisions with a known answer that the error rates below also count, and 7 readings of the CPSC's own photos, which count in neither rate; the error rates' 50 decisions are those 42 and 8 public runs: 7 settled in code with no model call, and 1, a photo of a mug, read by the model with the reviewers off.) Of the 7 that reached the reviewers, 2 had a known answer of no payment and the referee paid neither (0 of 2); 4 had a known answer to pay and it held 1 (1 of 4); the seventh was a reading of the CPSC's own photo, which counts in neither rate, and the referee sent it to a person. Each reviewer's own vote was kept in 1 row, where either alone would have paid what the referee paid; the sweep script now records both votes for the next run ([`src/components/evidence/reviewer-data.ts`](../src/components/evidence/reviewer-data.ts), [`/evidence/photo-checks#reviewers`](https://remedy-y0ym.onrender.com/evidence/photo-checks#reviewers)).
- **The two reviewers on one model and on two.** The claim path's own setting, run twice: first with both reviewers on Claude Opus 5.5, the default, then with the Remedy reviewer on Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`REMEDY_REVIEWER_MODEL_REMEDY`) and the Product reviewer unchanged. Both reviewers on Opus 5.5 against the Remedy reviewer on Sonnet 5.5: the same answer from both reviewers on 4 of 10 reviewed claims against 0 of 13; paid when it should not have been, 0 of 5 against 0 of 5; held when it could have been paid, 1 of 5 against 5 of 5; $0.0562 against $0.0541 a claim at list price. A second model made the pair no safer on these photos: neither run paid a photo it should not have (0 of 5 and 0 of 5), and with the Remedy reviewer on Claude Sonnet 5.5 the sample charger, the one photo here that should be paid, went to a person in every trial (5 of 5), so both reviewers stay on Claude Opus 5.5. The two reviewers' independence comes from the different rulebook each reads: on Claude Opus 5.5 they gave different answers on the look-alike hair dryer and the CPSC's hair dryer photo in all 6 claims they reviewed, and the same answer on the sample charger in all 4. The sample is small, 3 photos and 5 trials (15 claims a run), so it shows how the two settings behaved on these photos rather than a rate ([`content/verify/reviewer-models.json`](../content/verify/reviewer-models.json), counted by [`src/components/evidence/reviewer-models.ts`](../src/components/evidence/reviewer-models.ts); [`/evidence/photo-checks#reviewer-models`](https://remedy-y0ym.onrender.com/evidence/photo-checks#reviewer-models)).
- **Two error rates, on photos with a known answer** (counted by [`src/components/evidence/errors.ts`](../src/components/evidence/errors.ts) from the measured sweep, the recorded presets and the public runs, each model answer once):
  - **paid when it should not have been: 0 of 46** decisions, on 28 photos (copies of the official picture, look-alikes, another product, products not yet made safe, including the sample charger opened up with its cord still attached, and 10 photos by other photographers: intact laptop chargers and wall adapters, an unplugged cord, a cut cable with no charger, and an unrelated cable, each asked for a new photo). Across all four model settings in the sweep: 0 of 72;
  - **held for a person or a new photo when it could have been paid: 1 of 4**, on the owner-style photo with a known answer to pay (the sample charger with its cord cut: its recorded preset answer and three sweep trials). That is 4 decisions on one photo, the sample charger with its cord cut, whose right-product score of 0.80 to 0.82 is under the 0.85 pass mark, so only the two reviewers verify it: 3 of 4, and 1 went to a person at the firm. Three were paid through the two reviewers; in one trial the reviewers' votes did not settle it, so it went to a person at the firm, which pays nothing until that person decides. The CPSC's own published pictures, run with the official-picture match switched off to measure reading, count in neither rate: there the model alone would have paid for the recall's own picture in 3 of 7 runs, which is why the match runs first on every claim. The sweep ran three trials per photo ([`content/verify/sweep-results.json`](../content/verify/sweep-results.json), and [`content/verify/sweep-sample.json`](../content/verify/sweep-sample.json) for the sample recall's three photos at the claim path's setting); known answers are in [`content/verify/fixtures/cases.json`](../content/verify/fixtures/cases.json).
- **The sample recall's reference pictures.** With its earlier pictures (the whole charger and a small crop where the label had been removed), the sample charger with its cord cut scored 0.75 on the right-product check in all 4 runs. The sample recall now publishes what a firm would: the whole charger, where the cord meets the case, and the DC plug, all of the same public-domain charger. With these the score is 0.80 to 0.82 in 4 runs, still under the 0.85 pass mark, and 3 of the 4 were verified. Pass marks and prompts did not change ([`content/verify/sample-references.json`](../content/verify/sample-references.json), the earlier runs in [`content/verify/history/`](../content/verify/history/)).
- **Reading accuracy on real CPSC recalls: 16 of 18 readings right.** The CPSC's own photos of 3 recalled products made safe (recalls 26125 and 25007), sent with the official-picture match off, three trials each at the claim path's setting: right product 9 of 9, made safe 7 of 9. The model asked for a new photo in 5 of the 9, each time by the made-safe check: on the helmet in the trash (3 of 3) that is the right reading, since the recall asks for two photos and this one does not show the straps; on the hair dryer (2 of 3) it did not confirm the cut and its own-photo check came back unsure, having recognized the CPSC's published picture ([`/evidence/photo-checks`](https://remedy-y0ym.onrender.com/evidence/photo-checks#reading)).
- **Why the claim path uses Claude Opus 5.5 at medium effort.** On money, the sweep's four settings tie: none paid a photo it should not have (0 of 18 each). Scored against the known answers as they stood the day the sweep ran, the claim path's setting was right on **18 of 24** (Opus at low effort 20 of 24, Sonnet 5.5 19 of 24).
  - Re-scored with today's answers: 22 of 24, Opus at low effort and Sonnet 5.5 23 of 24, after two answers were revised, each dated with its case id and reason in [`content/verify/answer-changes.json`](../content/verify/answer-changes.json): the helmet in the trash (a new photo is the right answer, since the recall asks for two photos) and a look-alike (any answer but paying is right).
  - Held back can only be measured on an owner's photo that should be paid, and only this setting has run on one (the sample charger, paid in 3 of 4). Opus at low effort costs $0.0203 a check against $0.0297 and is the next to measure on owners' photos. The claim path stays on its setting because every recorded answer and the verification cache are keyed to it (`engineVersion()` in [`src/lib/verify/cache.ts`](../src/lib/verify/cache.ts) names the effort), so switching means a paid re-run of those recordings; the switch itself is one line, `effortFor()` in [`src/lib/verify/vision.ts`](../src/lib/verify/vision.ts) ([`/evidence/photo-checks`](https://remedy-y0ym.onrender.com/evidence/photo-checks#settings)).
- **A second line of care.** With the official-image match switched off, Claude Opus 5.5 still recognized the CPSC's example photo for recall 26125 as a downloaded picture rather than an owner's own photo ([`content/presets/recorded/cpsc-example.technical.json`](../content/presets/recorded/cpsc-example.technical.json)).
- **Measured:**
  - a photo check by Claude Opus 5.5 takes about 11 seconds: a median of 11.2 s, 6.4 to 20.7 s across 46 recorded live checks at the claim path's setting; when the product match is unclear, the two reviewers add a median of 10.7 s (8.6 to 13.9 s in 7 runs). Counted by `checkTimes()` in [`src/components/evidence/errors.ts`](../src/components/evidence/errors.ts) from the sweeps and the preset recordings;
  - 16 to 19 seconds from approval to a PayPal payout reaching SUCCESS, in 12 sandbox runs ([`content/public/measured.json`](../content/public/measured.json));
  - a sample-shop purchase refunded to its original PayPal capture at once, and a second claim on that purchase declined;
  - the two sandbox end-to-end runs, step by step with the PayPal ids and statuses each saw, in [`content/public/e2e-transcript.json`](../content/public/e2e-transcript.json) (written by `tests/e2e/claim.mjs` and `tests/e2e/claim-shop.mjs`, emails redacted).
