# Grounded in research

Part of the [Remedy README](../README.md). The published findings Remedy is built on, the problem measured from primary sources, and the pilot that would measure it with a real firm.

## The measured contrast

A recall claim portal today states its refund timing on its own page: "Once your registration is confirmed, your refund will be issued via check within 10-15 business days" ([Real Time Results, Cuisinart grill recall portal](https://www.recallrtr.com/SiteResources/3in1grill/locales/en/translation.json), read November 8, 2026). Remedy's PayPal payout reached the owner 17 to 18 seconds after approval, in 4 PayPal sandbox runs, and a refund to the original PayPal purchase went through at once ([`content/public/measured.json`](../content/public/measured.json)). The research below is why that speed, and a claim that asks for one photo, can move more recalled products out of homes.

## What is first here

Three things, each set beside the nearest existing product, portal or filing, read from its own page on November 8, 2026. The same three are on [`/evidence#first`](https://remedy-y0ym.onrender.com/evidence#first); the rows with every source and date are on [`/evidence/market#compare`](https://remedy-y0ym.onrender.com/evidence/market#compare) (`LANDSCAPE` in `src/components/evidence/Compare.tsx`).

1. **A photo of the product made safe, checked against a rule read from the official CPSC remedy text, then paid through PayPal in the same flow.** [Truepic Vision](https://www.truepic.com/blog/introducing-truepic-vision-for-consumer-product-safety-warranty-and-recall-claims) (product page, undated; partnership with a recall administrator announced February 10, 2025) has the owner take a fresh photo inside its own camera, checks the capture and matches the serial number, flags duplicate claims and unexpected locations, and leaves the decision to the firm's agents; it does not read the remedy into a rule, judge whether the product was made safe, or pay. Real Time Results portals have a team verify the photo and pay by check in 10 to 15 business days. Patent application [US 2016/0350756 A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20160350756A1/en) (filed May 29, 2015; abandoned April 6, 2018) describes a phone photo of a destroyed product and a receipt sent to a refund server, shown to refund approval personnel, with optional auto-approval for small or infrequent requests.
2. **A pool of photo hashes shared across firms, so a photo already paid at one firm is held at the next before any model call, with only a 64-bit hash and a row id crossing.** The [Truepic Risk Network](https://www.truepic.com/blog/introducing-the-truepic-risk-network) (September 25, 2025) shares anonymized device and capture signals across participating lenders and insurers; a recall administrator told the CPSC's 2026 docket on recall claims (June 8, 2026) that it now often requires live photo capture, which checks one claim at the moment it is made. Remedy compares the picture itself, as a hash, across recall firms and against every published recall picture, in open code.
3. **The refund routed back to the original PayPal capture inside PayPal's 180-day window, else a payout, with an unclaimed payout turned into a re-notice task in the firm's plan.** [Sedgwick's automotive recall page](https://www.sedgwick.com/product-recall/industries/automotive/) already lists PayPal and Venmo among its reimbursement options, so the rail is not new; its pages describe photo upload and reimbursement as separate steps worked by people. In auto insurance, [CCC Estimate-STP](https://ir.cccis.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ccc-introduces-next-generation-ai-based-photo-analysis) (November 2021) and Snapsheet send a photo-based estimate straight to payment on a confidence threshold; Remedy brings photo-in, money-out to recalls with a different gate, six checks in code and two reviewers, and adds the unclaimed-payout step that PayPal's `PAYMENT.PAYOUTS-ITEM.UNCLAIMED` event makes possible.

**The two reviewers, and where the design comes from.** The Product reviewer sees only the recall's official pictures; the Remedy reviewer sees only the remedy text. Each must cite what it saw, a referee in code counts only votes whose cited evidence checks out, and a disagreement goes to a person at the firm, who decides before anything is paid (`src/lib/verify/reviewers.ts`, `src/lib/verify/decide.ts`). The lineage: a model as the reviewer of another's output ([Zheng and others, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)); learning to defer the cases a model should not decide to a person ([Madras, Pitassi and Zemel, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06664)); a judge that answers only when confident and escalates the rest ([Jung, Brahman and Choi, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370)); a judge that retrieves evidence when unsure and abstains to human review when still unsure ([Badshah, Emami and Sajjad, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17994)); and the finding that nine frontier models from seven families made the same mistakes on the same items, so a panel of model names gave about two independent votes ([Kohli, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800)). That last finding is why the two reviewers here read two different rulebooks rather than two model names. "The agent proposes, a person approves" is a pattern many payment agents share this year and is not the headline.

## A 30-day pilot

One small firm with an open photo-refund recall runs every claim on that recall through Remedy for 30 days, from its own claim link, beside its current inbox. Two outcomes, each against the month before:

| Outcome | Baseline | Already recorded in |
|---|---|---|
| Time from photo to refund | The firm's median days from an emailed photo to its refund, from its own records for the 30 days before | Each claim's `claim.submitted` and `claim.paid` entries in the audit ledger and the payment's `paid_at`, shown in the firm's claims queue |
| Share of claims a person had to open | Every emailed photo, read by hand today | Each claim's decision in the `claim` table and the ledger's `claim.decided` entries, and the queue's Needs review view |

**Why it holds up:** an established tool that adds the same model gains a reader of photos; what makes each payout safe to send is the checks in code, the hash-chained evidence behind every payment, and the error rates measured on photos with known answers. It also grows with use: every claim photo is checked against the published pictures of every recall and every photo already claimed on any recall, at any firm, by a 64-bit hash alone, so each firm that joins makes every other firm's refunds safer.
